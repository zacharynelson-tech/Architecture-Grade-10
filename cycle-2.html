Cycle 2 — Site Orientation & Plot Plans

From the site you analyzed in Cycle 1, this cycle turns raw land into a placed, oriented building on paper — the first real design decision of the year.

10.4.410.4.110.2.4

Learning Objectives

Understand

Explain how a site plan differs from a plot plan in purpose and required content.

Apply

Apply standard annotation symbols (elevation tags, section callouts, dimensions) to a site drawing.

Create

Create a fully annotated site plan and plot plan for the assigned lot.

Lesson Outline · 9 Days

  • Day 1What Belongs on a Site Plan
    Walk through the full checklist a site plan must show — boundaries, north arrow, setbacks, utilities, contours. Compare a real site plan against the checklist.
  • Day 2Reading & Annotating a Site Plan
    Guided practice identifying and labeling each required item on a sample site plan.
  • Day 3Legal Descriptions & Boundaries
    Metes-and-bounds vs. survey plats vs. the rectangular survey system. Read a real legal description together.
  • Day 4Practice: Interpreting a Boundary Description
    Guided practice translating a written legal description into a rough boundary sketch.
  • Day 5Circulation & Access
    Pedestrian and vehicular circulation, parking requirements. Diagram circulation paths on a sample site.
  • Day 6Practice: Diagramming Circulation
    Guided practice laying out a driveway, walkway, and parking area on a provided site.
  • Day 7Site Orientation Principles
    Solar orientation, slope, and paving considerations that affect where a footprint sits on a lot.
  • Day 8Practice: Orienting a Footprint
    Guided practice placing and rotating a building footprint on the assigned site for solar exposure and access.
  • Day 9Workshop / Synthesis Day
    Supervised work day finalizing the Plot Plan. Preview Cycle 3.
